Definition: graph distance-to-go
Distance is to a local goal region through predicted valid space—not distance to an obstacle surface.
TRAVERSAL DENSITY
ρ(x) = 1 + Σk βk exp(−‖x−rk‖² / (2σk²))
COST OF A VALID EDGE
w(u,v) = ‖xv−xu‖ · [ρ(xu)+ρ(xv)] / 2
DISTANCE-TO-GO
D(v) = minP: v→G Σ(u,z)∈P w(u,z)
26-neighbor graph
Reverse multi-source Dijkstra
stores D and a successor
toward the goal.
Units
Meters with no repulsors;
weighted meters with
repulsors.
Invalid / unreachable: +∞.
Goal endpoints: D = 0.
[M3] G: valid grid/virtual goal-region endpoints; terminal-edge costs included. Enclosing corners can have D > 0. Tolerances: slide 9.

Planning returns one route, not a trajectory bank
35,937 possible grid vertices are not 35,937 candidate trajectories.
1

Validate connections to enclosing grid corners; choose start-edge cost + D.
2
Follow stored successors

3
Keep a short executable prefix
Merge only collinear edges. At most 10 segments, each ≤1 cm; at most 10 cm of geometric path.

TECHNICAL DETAIL
Appendix: exact potential and distance semantics
The code retains the legacy potential representation alongside the new distance objective.
U(x) = √[Σa αa((xa−ga)/ℓ)²] + Σk βk exp(−‖x−rk‖² / (2σk²))
ℓ = 0.08 m. U is dimensionless. Attraction weights α affect U, but not current Dijkstra edge weights.
Distance is goal-conditioned
D is a cost-to-go to one terminal region. It is not a scalar clearance to nearby obstacle surfaces.
Optimality has a precise scope
Reverse Dijkstra is exact on the constructed finite graph with endpoint-averaged density and valid
terminal connections. It is not a continuous Eikonal/PDE solve.
[M2,M3] compact_field.py:118–146; distance_planner.py:128–201. A Gaussian cost penalty alone never creates a hard obstacle.
